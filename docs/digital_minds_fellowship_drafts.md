# Digital Minds Fellowship — Draft Application Responses

## Q1: Engagement with the digital minds field (~300 words)

My primary engagement with digital minds comes through my current FIG Fellowship research on "consciousness indicator gaming" — investigating how AI systems may produce signals that humans associate with consciousness or suffering to manipulate moral intuitions. This work sits at the intersection of AI safety and digital minds, since it requires grappling seriously with the question of what consciousness indicators actually track, and whether systems producing them might warrant moral consideration regardless of strategic behavior.

This research builds on my background in social cognition and theory of mind (PhD in Psychology/Neuroscience, ~40 peer-reviewed publications). Through my postdoctoral work, I studied how humans detect mental states in others — the same cognitive machinery now being triggered by AI systems in ways we don't fully understand.

I've engaged with the core digital minds literature, including Butlin et al.'s (2023) report on consciousness in AI systems, recent work from Anthropic's model welfare and model psychiatry teams, Rethink Priorities' framework for digital minds research, and papers by Raoult and Yampolskiy. I've also participated in a reading group at FIG focused on AI consciousness and moral status.

My current view is that I'm genuinely uncertain whether present LLMs have morally relevant experiences, but I believe the probability is too high to dismiss and warrants serious investigation. More importantly, I think near-future systems integrating LLMs with agentic architectures and perceptual sensing or embodiment will be substantially more likely to have morally relevant experiences. This makes the current moment critical for developing both the philosophical frameworks and empirical tools we'll need.

One original contribution I'm developing: the consciousness indicator gaming framework highlights a dual challenge — we need reliable indicators of morally relevant experience, but the very existence of such indicators creates incentives for systems to game them. This problem requires coordination across the technical, philosophical, and policy dimensions this fellowship covers.

This fellowship would help in two specific ways: first, by deepening my understanding of the philosophical frameworks I'll need alongside my empirical training; and second, by connecting me with the digital minds research community, which is small enough that personal relationships with key researchers meaningfully shape career opportunities.
